Editor guide

The landing page

A phone or a tablet opens the reader app. A computer opens the experience's landing page instead: the book, its 3D hero, a QR code for the phone and the shop. The landing workspace shows that page with your draft.

What a computer gets

  • On a computer, every experience's address (/osct/, /demo/) shows its landing page, not the app. It loads at once, without the app.
  • The page has the experience's title and author, the hero – the book lies open on one scene, and one target's AR content rises from its print –, the QR code that opens the experience on a phone, the shop and a footer with the publisher and Privacy & terms.
  • The texts are in the visitor's language (English, French, Dutch or German).
  • Privacy & terms is a static page beside the app, at /privacy or /<experience>/privacy. The app links to the same page.
  • Readers get the landing page of the published version. Publish writes it (the version's landing.json).

The landing workspace

Open it with Landing in the top bar (next to Scenes, while an experience is open), with Landing page in the experience's Inspector (⚙ → landing page), or with Landing page in an experience's sheet on the Experiences page. Its address is /editor/<experience>/landing/; the top bar reads Experiences › experience › Landing. Scenes takes you back to the scene you had open.

  • The stage is the landing page a computer gets, with the draft – in the language the Inspector's texts section shows. Scroll it inside the stage. It is the reader's own page, so what you see is what is published next.
  • Inspector: the hero's scene and target, whether the stored hero is up to date, Refresh and Remove; the page's own texts and its shop.
  • Scenes: the same filmstrip as in a scene. The hero's scene is highlighted; a click on another card makes it the hero's scene.
  • History and Info work as in a scene. The panels can be moved, folded and closed; the layout is remembered for this workspace.

If the experience has plugins, the hero runs them in the editor's page. As on the scene's stage, the editor asks first before it runs code someone else saved; the page shows at once, the hero after your answer.

The publisher is not edited here yet; it comes from the experience's printed book.

Choosing the hero

The hero is one target with AR content – a model, a video or a component – on one scene: the book opens on that scene's surface and the content rises from the target's print. Marginalia and links cannot be the hero.

  • In the landing workspace: choose the scene in the Inspector; it takes its first target with AR content. Then choose the target. Only scenes and targets that can be the hero are listed. A click on a scene card does the same as choosing the scene.
  • On a scene's stage: right-click the target and choose Use for the landing page. It is greyed out, with the reason, on a target without AR content.

The choice saves itself, like every edit, and the stage shows the new hero. History lists it.

Without a choice the landing shows the experience's first model.

Up to date, out of date

The hero is stored with its picture, the content and the experience's looks when you choose it. The hero row says:

It saysWhat it means
up to dateThe stored hero is what the draft holds now.
out of dateThe content, a print, the looks or the print layout changed since. Refresh stores it again from the draft.
a problemThe target was removed or has no AR content any more. Choose another one.

Remove takes the choice away: after the next publish the landing shows the first model again.

The page's texts

The Inspector's texts section holds four texts of the page, per language: the intro under the title, get the app (the link under the intro and the heading beside the QR code), the QR line beside the QR code and the shop heading.

  • Switch the language with EN, DE, FR or NL above the fields – the reader's languages. The stage shows the page in that language.
  • An empty field shows the app's text for that language, greyed, and the page uses it. So a visitor only reads your text in the language you wrote it in; in the others they read the app's.
  • {title} and {author} are replaced by the experience's title and its book's author. The intro keeps its line breaks. The texts are plain text.
  • The field you type in is marked on the stage.
  • The QR code's description, Scan with your phone and Privacy & terms always stay the app's.

The app's own texts are part of the reader app (ui/landing/locales/ in the code). The intro is at most 600 characters, the QR line 300, the others 120.

The shop

The Inspector's shop section holds up to four items. Each has a picture, a label (the same in every language) and a link (an https:// address).

  • Until you change it, the page shows the app's two items – the book and the T-shirt –, greyed in the section. Edit makes them this experience's own.
  • + item adds one at the end; ✕ removes one. An item without a label or with a link that is not an address is not saved yet – the top bar says why.
  • Click an item's picture: Library… opens the Library in the Inspector to choose one of the experience's images; The book's cover shows the print layout's front cover; No picture leaves it empty.
  • Remove every item and the page has no shop. Use the app's items brings the app's two items back.

Every change saves itself and shows on the stage at once; History lists it. Readers get it with the next publish. Texts and the shop do not need a hero: without one, the landing shows the experience's first model.

A scene cannot be called landing

landing is the landing workspace's address, so no scene can have that id. New scenes get ids like scene-3; an AI agent or the API that asks for landing is refused.
