Editor guide

The stage

The stage shows the open scene on a desk: its surfaces, the printed images and the AR content, playing live as in the reader. Every target counts as found.

Moving the camera

  • Drag orbits, all the way round: down to the level of the table, below it to look up, over the top to the far side.
  • Shift + drag, the middle button or two fingers pan.
  • Wheel or pinch changes the distance.
  • Double tap returns to the reader's view: 35 cm above the page, tilted 30°.

Picking

A click selects what lies under the pointer, as the select filters allow. A click on nothing selects the scene. Hovering shows a grey outline of what a click would pick; the selection has an orange one.

A right click opens the menu for what lies there: add a node, remove it, edit the placement, add a target, Replay the first find, the view settings.

The gizmo

The handles work in the node's own space, so a drag changes its placement relative to its parent. The drag is saved when it ends.

A target is moved differently: select it, switch placement on in the Inspector, then drag its printed image on the stage; the small square at its corner sets the printed width.

Grids and snapping

  • Surface grid: the targets' grid on each surface, with a spacing in mm. A surface can have its own.
  • Floor grid: the net on the table, 50 mm by default. Surfaces snap to it.
  • Vertical grid: the same net standing, for walls. Off by default.
  • Target marks: frames every target's printed place with its id, to check places against the print.

Snap is a switch in the same menu, off at first. Holding Shift or Ctrl during a drag turns it around for that drag. Moving snaps to axes, other objects, outlines or the grid; rotating to 15° steps; scaling to 0.1 steps.

The origin (view menu) is the point x and y of a target's placement are counted from: a corner, an edge's centre or the centre of the surface. What is saved stays mm from the surface's top left corner.

What the stage does not show

  • The reader's interface around the scene, and opened entries.
  • A reader's stored progress: the stage's progress lasts while the workspace is open.
  • A goto to another scene: the stage stays on its scene and logs it.
