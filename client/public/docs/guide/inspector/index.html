Editor guide

The Inspector

The Inspector shows what is selected and saves every change by itself. Its rows come from the content model, so a plugin's parameters look like built-in ones.

A target

  • Image: the printed image. Library… picks one, Upload… adds one.
  • Placement: switch it on to see and edit where the image is printed: surface, x, y and width in mm. On the stage the image gets handles while it is on.
  • Recognition: an optional prepared frame that helps tracking. Original only keeps this target on its printed image.
  • AR content: a link down to the target's entity.

The head is a breadcrumb: target › entity › node. A click on a crumb selects that level.

An entity or a node

  • Name: what the tree shows, and how rules address the node.
  • + node: add a group, model, video, image, text or light, or a plugin's node type. Add as: next to the selected node or inside it.
  • Placement: position, rotation, scale, relative to the parent. Uniform links the scale axes.
  • File: the model, video or image, from the Library or uploaded. A credits line belongs to it.
  • Textures and levels: the same file in several sizes; the reader picks one by quality.
  • Material: the node's own, or one a plugin registers.
  • Components: each with its rows and remove. Add component is at the bottom.
  • make it code: turns the node into a plugin node type you can edit in the Code tab.

Numbers are scrub fields: press and slide, or click to type. Shift ×10, Alt ×0.1. A small arrow resets a value to its default.

An entry

Select an entry row in the tree. Its form replaces the Inspector:

  • Title, category, scene, author, tags. Choosing another scene moves the entry there.
  • Text with a live preview. A blank line starts a paragraph; *emphasis*, **strong** and [links](https://…) work.
  • Variables in the text: {hasKey: The door opens.|It is locked.} shows one text or the other. The preview uses the stage's current values.
  • Picture, caption, link.

entry… in the tree's + add menu makes an entry without a target: it shows in the reader's list only.

The scene

With the scene selected, the Inspector has collapsible sections:

  • Scene: label, group, targets, arrange the surfaces in a row. The surfaces themselves are in the tree. Scenes and surfaces
  • Post-processing: passes over the rendered picture.
  • Rules: the scene's own rules.
  • Narrative: one line with the count of variables and events, and links to the Variables and Log tabs.

Post-processing

The experience has one list of passes that every scene shows. A scene may have its own list instead; the section's head says which you are editing.

Add pass: bloom, vignette, grade, or a plugin's pass. Each pass folds, has an on / off switch and arrows for its place in the chain. More than three passes or more than one bloom is too much for a phone; the section says so.

Variables and Log

Two tabs beside the Inspector test the narrative. Long lists are sections that open with a click.

  • Log: tap – a button per target taps it as a reader would. Below, one timeline, newest first: the stage's events, the rules that ran (behaviour, with what they did), the plugins' log lines and errors. The problems – the Logic panel's errors and warnings, plugins not on the stage – sit above it; a click goes to the box or the plugin. Chips show only the kinds you pick. Clear empties the timeline.
  • Variables: every variable by scope with its type and value; change one to test a branch. Set makes a new one. Reset starts a new game.

The experience

Click the ⚙ next to the experience's name in the top bar. The Inspector shows its title, the printed book switch with author and publisher, the experience's post-processing (the list every scene without its own shows), its appearance, and at the end its own rules and declared variables.

Appearance

Three moments, each with a look: reveal (the first find), found (every later find) and lost. Built-in looks: inflateDissolve, printLift, fade; plugins can add more. Play in a moment's head shows it on the stage.

Below them, card: how the card of a glossary entry flies from the page to the screen when the reader finds it. The built-in cardFlight peels, spins and slaps; its rows set the flight time, how much of the screen it fills, the lift, the spins, the impact and the vibration. A plugin can bring its own card look. Play flies a shown target's card on the stage.
