Editor guide

Experiences, import, publish

The start page lists the experiences here and is where one comes in. Publishing happens from inside an experience.

The Experiences page

Your experiences are on the left, the right column shows what you are doing:

  • Nothing selected: quick access – New experience, Import, and Last worked on: the places you and others worked on last, across all experiences (book · scene · when · who). Click one to open it there.
  • A click on a cover selects the experience (it is outlined) and shows its sheet. Double-click it, or press Enter, to open it in the editor. The ··· under a cover selects it too.
  • The arrow keys move the selection, Esc clears it.

The ☰ menu at the top right has New experience… and Import… as well.

A new experience

New experience (the quick access card, the dashed + card or the ☰ menu) goes step by step in the right column:

  1. Start: Empty – one scene, nothing on it – or From an export – a file, a URL or a published version (below).
  2. Name: a title and the address (the slug). The whole reader address is shown – https://<installation>/<slug>/ – and the slug is proposed from the title: lower-case letters, digits and dashes. A few words are taken by the site itself (editor, api, docs, scene, …) and every experience needs its own.
  3. Create: the experience is made and opens in the editor.

The address can't be changed later: once the experience is published, printed QR codes and shared links depend on it.

Importing an experience

Import brings an experience in as a new one – it is New experience → From an export. Three sources:

  • File: an export file (an experience's Export – ….osct.json, with its plugin code), or a game.config.json from your computer.
  • URL: a game.config.json, or a content base – an experience's folder on the CDN that holds latest.json, e.g. …/osct/staging/demo.
  • Published: the version of an experience this installation has published.

Assets at (for a bare game.config.json) is where the media paths resolve. No media is copied: the draft points at the files where they are.

Before anything is imported the column shows what comes in: the build, its date, the app and schema version and what it holds. Next goes on to the name – the title starts as the export's.

An experience's sheet

Select an experience and the column shows its sheet: title, author and reader address, and

  • Open: the experience in the editor.
  • Export: the draft as one file (<slug>-<build>.osct.json) – its content, its list of files and its plugin code. Import reads it back, here or on another installation. The media stay on the CDN; the file names them.
  • Replace…: replace the draft with an export, a URL or a published version, step by step – the source, then a comparison, then Replace the draft. Readers keep the live version until you publish.
  • Versions: every publish – when, where, who – and which one readers get now (live).

Make live brings an older version of this installation back: Make the version of … live on staging again? Readers get it on their next start. The draft is not changed. Every published version is kept, so any of them can come back; the list then shows the switch as made live again. Production is made live from its own editor.

Publishing

Publish is the cloud button in the top bar of an open experience. Next to it: draft only or live on ….

It opens one box in the middle of the page and asks first: Publish “…” to staging?, what readers get and when it was last published. Publish starts it; Cancel, Esc or a click beside the box closes it. If a saved plugin source does not compile, the box says so in red and Publish stays off.

While it runs the box says Writing the build…, then Live for readers ✓ – readers have the new state from that moment – and counts the export archive (Archive 412 / 1522). Keep the page open until it says Published, with how many files were new. It closes by itself after two seconds; Open reader opens the experience.

If the logic has errors or warnings, the box names how many and the first one; with errors the button says Publish anyway.

  • An installation publishes to its own environment only: a local machine to local, staging to staging, production to production. There is no choice.
  • Each experience has its own store. Publishing one never touches another.
  • No CDN connected means this server stores and serves the published content itself.
  • Publish carries the draft and every saved plugin source. Unsaved code stays behind.
  • A big experience takes a while – mostly the export archive, written after readers already have the build. Leaving the page while it runs asks first. If a publish is already running (another tab, after a reload), Publish shows that one.

Going back: revert the draft in History, then publish again.

Readers open every experience at its own address, /<slug>/ – /osct/, /demo/. The slug is chosen when the experience is made (New experience: a title and an address; the column shows the reader's address in full) and can't be changed once it is published – printed QR codes depend on it. Older codes and links without an experience still work: they open the installation's default experience, and the book's code its own.

AI connections

An AI agent (Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex CLI, OpenCode, Cursor) can work in the editor as you. It saves drafts and never publishes.

  • Connect an AI agent: pick the agent, press Create token & copy prompt, paste the prompt into the agent. It sets itself up.
  • Existing connections: each token with when it was last used and when it expires. Renew gives it another 182 days; Revoke stops it at once.

A token is shown once, so every agent gets its own. Details: MCP server.
