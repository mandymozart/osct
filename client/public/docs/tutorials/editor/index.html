Tutorials for editors

Twelve short jobs, each a few minutes. They use the demo experience, so nothing here can break a real one.

1 · A first look with the demo

  1. Open /editor, sign in, and click the cover of The Demo Book. If it is not there, import it first.

  2. In the Scenes panel at the bottom, click the scene 1–2. Drag on the stage to orbit, use the wheel to zoom.

  3. In the tree, click model under The key. The Inspector shows its placement and its components.

  4. Click the scene's row (1–2) at the top of the tree. Open the Log tab (the log link in the scene's Inspector) and press the tap button The key. The key rattles, hasKey becomes true, and the timeline lists what happened.

  5. Open scene 3–4. Because hasKey is true, the door opens. Press Restart in the Tools to start over: the door stays shut and shows its hint.

2 · Add a target with AR content

A target is a printed image plus what appears on it.

  1. Open the scene the image is printed on. At the end of the tree, click + add and choose target….

  2. Fill in the form: a title, the printed image (Upload… or Library…), the content that appears on it (a model, video, image, text, group, light, or none yet) with its file, and listed in: the category the reader's menu shows it under.

  3. Set where it is printed: the surface, x · y and the width in mm. Or drag the translucent image on the stage and pull its corner square for the width.

  4. Create it. The stage shows the target with its content at once.

  5. Select the content's node. Use Move (W), Rotate (E), Scale (R), or type values in the Inspector. A video or image lies flat by default; rotation X = 90° stands it up.

Measure the printed place on the real page. If the printed width is wrong, the content has the wrong size in the reader. A scene takes at most 10 targets.

3 · Write an entry's text

  1. Click the entry's row in the tree (the one with the page icon, above its target). Its form opens in the Inspector.

  2. Type the text. Leave a blank line for a new paragraph. Use *emphasis*, **strong** and [a link](https://example.org). The preview below shows it as the reader will.

  3. To make the text depend on what the reader did, use a variable: {hasKey: You hold the key.|Find the key first.}

  4. Add a picture with its caption, or a link, if the entry has one. Everything saves itself.

For a text without a printed image, e.g. a glossary term, use + add › entry… in the tree.

4 · Make something react: a rule

Goal: a tap on a model plays its clip and remembers that it happened.

  1. Select the model's node. If it has a clip, add the component animation at the bottom of the Inspector, choose the clip and set plays on to never.

  2. Add the component behaviour. In rule 1 press tap.

  3. + action… → play, and choose the clip.

  4. + action… → set: name tapped, value true, scope experience so it is kept with the reader's progress.

  5. Test it: open the Log tab, press the target's tap button. The timeline shows a behaviour line.

  6. Use it elsewhere: on another node add a behaviour with found, when tapped, and an action such as show.

More: the logic system. To see the rules as a graph, open the Logic panel.

5 · Add a scene

  1. In the Scenes panel, click the dashed + at the end (or the add button in its head, which adds after the open scene).

  2. Choose a preset: double page, single page or wall, and give it a label, e.g. 12–13.

  3. Drag its card to its place in the order.

  4. Select its surface in the tree and set a reference image: a picture of the printed page. It shows on the stage so you can place targets where they are printed.

  5. Add the targets (tutorial 2).

6 · Give the picture a look

  1. Set the shading in the Tools to rendered. Passes show only there.

  2. For every scene: click the ⚙ next to the experience's name in the top bar; under post-processing press + bloom, + vignette or + grade and tune the rows.

  3. For one scene only: select the scene, open post-processing, press own for this scene, then change its list. Back to the experience's drops the override.

  4. How things appear: in the experience's Inspector, under appearance, pick a look for found (e.g. printLift) and press play to see it.

Keep to three passes and one bloom. More is too slow on a phone, and the editor warns.

7 · Publish, check, take back

  1. Check the top bar says Saved. If you changed code, press Save in the Code tab too.

  2. Press Publish (the cloud button), then Publish in the box. Wait for Published – readers have it from Live for readers ✓ on.

  3. Open the reader on a phone: /<experience>/ – every experience at its own address, /osct/, /demo/. Point it at the printed page.

  4. Something wrong? Give readers the version before first (tutorial 10). Then open History (the clock in the Tools), click the change, choose Revert or Jump back to here, and publish again.

8 · Let an AI agent help

  1. On the Experiences page, under Connect an AI agent, choose your agent.

  2. Press Create token & copy prompt and paste the prompt into the agent. It installs the connection and asks you to restart it.

  3. Ask it something small first: “List the scenes of the demo.”

  4. After each change it gives you a link. Open it and check the stage. Publishing stays your button.

What an agent can do: MCP server.

9 · Start a new experience

  1. On the Experiences page, click New experience in the right column (or the dashed + card).

  2. Start: choose Empty, then Next. (From an export starts from a file, a URL or a published version instead.)

  3. Name: type the title. The address under it is proposed from the title; change it if you want a shorter one. Read the reader's address below the field: this is what goes into QR codes, and it can't be changed once the experience is published.

  4. Create. The editor opens the new experience on its one scene, an empty double page.

  5. Go on with a scene and a target. Nothing reaches readers before you publish.

A try-out you no longer need: tutorial 11.

10 · Roll back to an older version

Readers got something broken. Give them the version before, then fix the draft in peace.

  1. On the Experiences page, press Manage under the experience.

  2. Under Versions, find the last good one. ● live marks what readers have now.

  3. Click make live in its row, read the question, press Make live. Readers get that version on their next start.

  4. Now fix the draft: Open the experience, and revert the change in History – or put the good version into the draft with Replace… › Published.

  5. Publish again when it is right. That is a new version; the others stay in the list.

Make live changes what readers get, never the draft. It works for this installation's own environment. More

11 · Delete an experience

For a try-out or a duplicate. It cannot be undone.

  1. On the Experiences page, press Manage under the experience.

  2. Press Export and keep the file. It is the only way back.

  3. Press Delete experience… at the end of the sheet and read the red box: what goes, and whether it is live.

  4. Type the experience's address (e.g. night-garden) into the field and press Delete for good.

If it is live, readers keep getting the published build after the delete, and nobody can change that from the editor any more. Do not delete an experience to take it away from readers. More

12 · Choose the landing page's hero

On a computer, readers get the experience's landing page. Its hero is one target with AR content, rising from the open book.

  1. Open the demo and press Landing in the top bar. The stage shows the landing page with the draft; scroll it to see the QR code, the shop and the footer.

  2. If the editor asks whether to run the experience's plugin code, answer as on the scene's stage. The hero starts after your answer.

  3. In the Inspector, choose a scene. Only scenes with a model, a video or a component are listed; the scene takes its first such target. Or click a scene card in the Scenes panel.

  4. Choose the target. The hero row says saving…, then up to date, and the stage shows the new hero.

  5. Back in the scene (Scenes in the top bar), change the hero's content. The landing's Inspector now says out of date: press Refresh.

  6. Publish. A computer gets the new landing page.

On a scene's stage, a right click on a target and Use for the landing page chooses it too. More
