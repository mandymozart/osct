4 · Free = our brand

Free experiences pay with visibility: our mark is the marketing, the invitation at the end is the funnel.

  • Their brand stays in charge of the content (title, colours, logo); ours is a quiet, fixed layer the free tier cannot switch off.
  • Three touch points: splash line, a small mark while scanning, the "make your own" card at the end and in Info.
  • Never in the camera picture over the artwork, never an ad – the data-sovereignty promise holds for free too.
  • The free tier is the platform's self-service entry: a sign-up, an editor reduced to the poster case, and limits (frame 10).
Decide: is "own brand" a contract only, or also a paid plan anyone can switch on (frame 10)?

5 · Who has access

Today roles are global (reader / editor / admin, #65). White label needs them per experience.

  • Owner – a person or an organisation; exactly one per experience; transfers, deletes, links domains.
  • Admin – people and everything in it. Editor – content, publish to staging. Designer – brand and looks. Reviewer – reads the draft.
  • Instance admins see every experience of their instance; the platform (us) sees none of the content unless invited – data sovereignty.
  • Readers: public, code (book copies, tickets), or invited. Codes reuse the existing passes idea.
  • The MCP / AI tokens act with exactly the person's role (as now).
Decide: the role list (five, or Owner/Editor/Reviewer only?); production publish – owner and admin only?

6 · Ownership and custom domains

An experience always has its instance path and may add its own domains.

  • Own domain = CNAME to our edge + a TXT to prove it; TLS automatic (Kamal proxy / Let's Encrypt).
  • One primary address – the one in the manifest and the install. Others redirect to it.
  • Ownership is the legal and data side: who decides, whose readers. Transfers move the role, not the instance.
  • Moving instances is the "instance at a click" path (STRATEGY phase C).
  • Per-host brands only after this (research pitfall 20): host mapping must be config only.
Decide: own domains per experience, or per instance only for now? Do reader accounts move on transfer, or do readers re-consent?

8 · Newsletters

Today: one fixed option "Receive updates from Building Fictions" (#325). White label: lists per instance and per experience.

  • The reader sees only the lists of the experience they are in plus the instance's own.
  • Consent per list, written by the reader only – admins read, never write (RULES, user-centric data).
  • Free tier: the artist's list and ours, separate, both off by default.
  • Sign-in mails take the experience's title and colours (fixes research pitfall 18).

10 · Plans and limits

Free versions are real but small; each limit is one the platform can enforce cheaply.

  • Limits the core can count today: experiences, targets, media size (content store), editors. Scans need arrivals per experience (research pitfall 19).
  • Never cut readers off mid-scan: a soft limit with a friendly page, a grace month.
  • Expiry for free posters keeps hosting costs bounded – posters are short-lived anyway.
  • Payments: none yet – a plan switch by an admin first, a payment provider later.
Decide: the free limits; whether paid plans exist now or only free + contract for the start.

11 · Poster XR (first use case)

Tilman, 2026-11-22: "via qr.viennastruggle.at I want to provide free poster XR. we can use the platform to host it."

  • One instance on our platform, on qr.viennastruggle.at (a DNS record to the Kamal host, its own DB and CDN folder).
  • One poster = one experience with one target and one video entity. The existing core already does all of it (editor targets, video on found, publish).
  • What is new: a sign-up for creators, the four-step poster editor (a reduced view of the editor), the free limits, the "made with Vienna Struggle" layer, the landing page.
  • Readers need no account; the QR opens the poster's page, the camera starts after one tap.
  • Moderation: public posters on our domain need terms, a report link and a way to take one down.
Decide: creators sign up themselves, or invited only for the first months? Who moderates?
